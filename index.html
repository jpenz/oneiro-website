MEET THE GROUP

The four behind the kefi.

Two lead vocalists, bouzouki, keyboards, guitar, and drums — and the showmanship, stage presence, and kefi that put Oneiro a level above.

Yianni

Keyboards · Lead Vocals · Founding Member

Yianni began his musical career at an extremely young age and grew into an exceptional musician, mastering a wide variety of instruments. Though his true love is the piano, he also plays guitar, bouzouki, drums, and percussion. Originally trained in jazz and musical theory, his special fondness for Greek music shaped the group's unique sound. As a founding member, his dedication is matched only by his unbelievable performing and solid lead vocals.

Andreas

Bouzouki · Vocals

Originally a classical pianist — mastering Chopin, Rachmaninoff, Gershwin and more — Andreas performed these works in front of many audiences before picking up his first bouzouki at a young age. Self-taught, he favors the great bouzouxithes: Manolis Chiotis, Manolis Karandinis, and of course Tsitsanis. His love and passion for the bouzouki is evident every moment he is on and off the stage, and his interpretations of Greek music are both unique and appealing.

Dimitris

Guitar · Founding Member

Dimitris owes much of his skill on the guitar to his father, who started him on the instrument — he essentially grew up with a guitar in his hands. He found his true passion experimenting with blues and rock, and upon co-founding Oneiro he merged those techniques into a lively, smooth style of rhythm guitar. His rhythmic style is the backbone of the group's groove and drive.

Sotiris

Drums · Percussion

Sotiris began performing at a young age, sitting in with bands throughout his youth and playing live alongside his father and uncle — accomplished dimotiko musicians. More than a decade ago he took charge of his growth and trained under the group's keyboardist, Yianni. After years of practice, dedication, and discipline he became an exceptional musician — a true asset to the group, and the heartbeat of its rhythm and groove.

THE MUSIC

Whatever your night needs — we know it.

A vast repertoire across every Greek tradition. We open soft, build through the night, and close on the floor. Tap any style to go deeper.

Laïka The big-room sound — bouzouki, vocals, and the songs everyone knows. +

The heart of a Greek night. Sweaty bouzouki, tsifteteli, and the laïka everyone in the room can sing — the songs that keep the floor full until the lights come up.

We playKazantzidis · Dalaras · Tsaligopoulou · Mitropanos
Dimotika Mainland folk — heavy clarino, heavy footwork. +

Mainland folk music: the kalamatianó, tsamikó, and syrtó your family has danced for generations. Driving clarino and a rhythm section built for the circle.

We playAidonidis · Samiou · Pyrgaki · Chalkias
Nisiótika Island music from the Cyclades and Dodecanese. +

Island music from the Cyclades and Dodecanese — violin and laoúto, the kind of set that ends with the whole room in one big circle.

We playParios · Konitopoulou · Mariza Koch · Oikonomidis
Paradosiaká Traditional Greek music in its broadest sense. +

Traditional Greek music in the broadest sense — the regional songs of the mainland and islands, from Pontian to Epirote to Asia-Minor, dating back centuries. Where every other style on this list comes from.

RegionsPontian · Epirote · Asia Minor · Smyrnaic
