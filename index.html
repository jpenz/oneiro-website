REPERTOIRE

Whatever your night needs — we know it.

Six traditions across one set. We open with kalamatianó, push into laïka by ten, slow it down with the nisiótika after dinner, and close with the dimotika nobody's got the legs for. Tap any style to go deeper.

ΛΑΪΚΑ Laïka The big-room stuff. Sweaty bouzouki, tsiftetelia until 4 a.m. +

The big-room stuff. Sweaty bouzouki, tsiftetelia until 4 a.m., songs everyone in the room knows the words to.

Instruments Bouzouki · Vocals · Drums · Keys
We play Kazantzidis · Dalaras · Tsaligopoulou · Mitropanos
ΔΗΜΟΤΙΚΑ Dimotiká Mainland folk. Heavy clarino, heavy footwork. +

Mainland folk. Heavy clarino, heavy footwork. The dances your yiayia taught you — kalamatianó, tsamikó, syrtó.

P.S.Our drummer bounces so hard during a tsamikó you can't tell if he's fucking or getting fucked.

Instruments Clarino · Laoúto · Violí · Defi
We play Aidonidis · Samiou · Pyrgaki · Chalkias
ΝΗΣΙΩΤΙΚΑ Nisiótika Island music from the Cyclades and Dodecanese. +

Island music from the Cyclades and Dodecanese. Violin, laoúto, the kind of set that ends with everyone in a circle.

Instruments Violí · Laoúto · Tsampoúna · Santoúri
We play Parios · Konitopoulou · Mariza Koch · Oikonomidis
ΠΑΡΑΔΟΣΙΑΚΑ Paradosiaká The umbrella. Where every other category on this list comes from. +

The umbrella. Greek traditional music in the broadest sense — the dimotiká tragoúdia of mainland and islands, dating back to Byzantine times. Where every other category on this list comes from before it had a name.

Instruments Voice · Laoúto · Outi · Daoúli
Traditions Pontian · Epirote · Mikrasiátiko · Smyrnaiko
ΣΚΥΛΑΔΙΚΟ Skiládiko The decadent branch of laïko. We bring that energy when the night needs to break the rules. +

The decadent branch of laïko — the music of the cheap, smoky, shady night clubs on the edge of town. Heavily amplified bouzouki and electric guitar on a raised stage. Karras and Pantazis lit it up in the '70s and '80s. We bring that energy to a private room when the night needs to break the rules.

P.S.Keys and lead vocal will burn a cigarette on your μουνί during a solo.

Instruments Electric Bouzouki · Electric Guitar · Keys · Vocals
We play Karras · Pantazis · Terzís · Makropoulos
ΡΕΜΠΕΤΙΚΑ Rebétika The foundation. We respect the lineage. +

The foundation. Urban song from the 1920s–1950s — bouzouki and baglamás, smoke and exile. Vamvakaris, Tsitsanis, Bellou, Papaioannou. We respect the lineage; the older bookers know we do.

P.S.Stay after the gig to smoke a joint with the guitar and bouzouki players.

Instruments Bouzouki · Baglamás · Voice · Guitar
We play Vamvakaris · Tsitsanis · Bellou · Papaioannou

THE CREW

The four behind the kefi.

Two lead vocalists, bouzouki, keys, guitar, drums. They grew up through the church halls, the festivals, and the late nights — and they've been playing rooms together since before any of you were ready to dance.

Bouzouki

Andreas Karantsinides

Bouzouki · Vocals

Pretty Thessaloniki boy. The face most likely to get followed home from a gig. Trained on classical piano — Chopin, Rachmaninoff, Gershwin — then taught himself bouzouki by ear, with Chiotis and Tsitsanis on repeat. Day job: dentist. He'll polish your mouth out before or after the bouzouki does, depending on what you're into.

Yianni Shafkalis

Keyboards · Vocals · Founding member

Cypriot mother, Cretan father — generations of islanders whose family trees forgot to branch out. The trade-off: a couple rare blood disorders, perfect pitch, and the kind of musical recall the gene pool produces when it's small enough to drink from. Theory-grounded, jazz-trained, 150+ students taught. The conservatory inside the band.

Dimitris Penz

Acoustic · Electric Guitar

Pindus mountain blood. Family line traces back to klephts who taught the Ottomans about ambushes, and grandfathers who measure time in bear maulings. Learned guitar from his father between tsipouro runs across borders that don't exist on any current map. Rhythm-forward style — the silence between the notes is in the genes, too.
